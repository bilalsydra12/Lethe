02

History card: what really happened

Fact

The Lost Colony

In 1587 about 115 English men, women and children settled on Roanoke Island, in what is now North Carolina, led by John White. His granddaughter, Virginia Dare, was born there that August: the first English child born in the Americas. White sailed home for supplies, and war with Spain kept him away for three years.

When he returned in August 1590, on Virginia's third birthday, the colony was gone. The houses had been taken down and a palisade built. Carved into a gatepost was one word, CROATOAN, the name of a nearby island and its people. The colonists had agreed to carve a cross if they left in danger. There was no cross. Nobody has ever found them.

Our story

Why they're here

Roanoke is the one Thin Place the Sealers failed to close in time. The colonists followed their dreams through it and became the first layer of the forgotten. Since then, everyone who has vanished without a trace has washed up in this fog: sailors, lighthouse keepers, travellers from every century.

The colony looks the way the colonists remember it, not the way White found it: houses standing, tables set, as if everyone stood up at once. Because nobody living remembers them, time here doesn't move. A cut in wood stays fresh for four hundred years.

06

Side stories and the choice

Every side story returns a name to someone who has nearly lost it, and gives back a memory or a keepsake in return. The people are real; their fates in Lethe are our story.

Side story · the Dare House

The girl who was born here

A three-year-old girl sits under a table, singing a lullaby she half remembers. Her mother, Eleanor Dare, has kept a signal fire at the landing for four hundred years, waiting for her own father to come back. Find the missing words of the lullaby around the colony and sing it back.

Reward
A memory returned · Keepsake: the christening spoon (longer parry window)
History
Virginia Dare, born August 1587
Side story · the Lighthouse

Three keepers

On the sand spit, a lighthouse whose lamp has gone out. Three keepers argue on the stairs about whose turn it is. Relight the lamp with the UV Lantern and a mirror puzzle, and they remember their names.

Reward
A star · Keepsake: the oilskin (quieter steps)
History
Flannan Isles, Scotland: three keepers vanished in December 1900
Side story · gated, returns later

The ship that sailed without them

A brigantine drifts offshore with its lamps lit and dinner on the table. A captain, his wife and their little girl stand on deck. You can't reach them until you can call a crowd of names at once (Voice: Chorus).

Reward
A star · Keepsake: the ship's compass (points to the nearest star)
History
The Mary Celeste, found empty in December 1872
The choice: the fire at the landing. The colonists are split. Eleanor Dare wants her signal fire lit, so they can be found. Master Hollis (a fictional colonist) and the others want it put out: being found is how the Sealers find you. Light it and the Hollows grow restless (harder fights here), but Eleanor fights beside you against the Bellringer and returns in Part Three. Put it out and the colony goes quiet (easier fights, a keepsake from Hollis), but Eleanor stops speaking to you, and the lullaby side story is lost.

08

The cross-time puzzle at the post

THEN · Scene 17 · Eesa, 14

The post is clean except for one word. The player moves Eesa's hands over it and chooses where to carve his first star: nine possible spots (three heights on three faces). The game records the choice silently. Nothing tells the player it matters.

NOW · step 5 · Nysa

Fifteen years later the post is covered in hundreds of carvings and grey plaster. The Lantern reveals only fresh cuts. The player has to remember where they carved it. The star is there, exactly, and the wood is still pale. Most players won't expect their own choice to come back. Hint ladder: after 90 seconds, Eesa's notebook shows a sketch of the post.

09

Boss: the Bellringer

Built from the colony itself: house timber, ship rope and sailcloth, with the colony's great bronze bell for a head. Its weapon is forgetting: every ring takes something from the player. Arena: the square, about 40 m across, with the post at its centre, the well, and four porches for cover. About 8–12 minutes on Balanced.

PHASE 1The Ring100–60%

It rings every 20 seconds. Each ring takes one thing away for 10 seconds, in order: dodge, then the Lantern, then the screen (the health bar and edges fade).

  • Rope lash: a wide sweep; jump it or duck behind a porch.
  • Lantern drag: pulls burning lanterns across the ground, leaving lines of fire.
  • Stomp: knocks you down if you're close.
  • Weak points: three rope joints on its legs. Carve them with the chisel (each costs a small memory) to bring it to its knees, then shoot the bell with the caliver for big damage.
PHASE 2The Choir60–25%

It pulls Hollows out of the fog and rings every 12 seconds.

  • The answer is the post. Stand at CROATOAN and call the names you've learned in the colony. Each Hollow you name stops, turns, and grabs the Bellringer's legs.
  • If you lit Eleanor's fire, Eleanor Dare joins the fight here.
  • Players who skipped the side stories have fewer names, so this phase is harder for them. The game rewards exploring.
PHASE 3The Price25–0%

It tears free and lowers the bell over the square to crush it. The cold open replays: Nysa knocked flat, the chisel, one memory offered.

  • "The night Eesa showed me Saturn." Hold to spend. This time the player lived that night in Scene 6, and Eesa's voice says the line they heard him say then.
  • The bell cracks from top to bottom. The fog clears for the first time: there is no sky behind it, only darkness and seven faint stars.
Rewards: the chisel technique Anchor (carve a name into a place: checkpoint and fast travel), a bronze shard from the bell (Mahl Stick Tier II if not yet upgraded), and the Square becomes the colony's first anchor. On Story difficulty the rings take less away; on Grounded a hit while the screen is gone also costs your next memory card.

Build notes for Unreal Engine

AreaNotes
ScaleLane width 3–4 m; cottage 6 × 4 m, door 1.9 m; palisade 4 m high; bell tower 14 m; Bellringer 9 m standing. Player sprint about 6 m/s.
Fog and lightVolumetric fog everywhere, densest at the edges so the playable space reads; Lumen; warm point lights in windows and lanterns as the only colour; nothing above the fog (black).
MaterialsScanned timber, thatch, wet mud, iron, weathered bronze (Fab / Megascans). Everything slightly damp.
SoundThe bell is the score (low A). Whispered names placed on the frozen colonists. Unseen surf on the west side.
First build targetGrey-box the whole level (steps 1–11) and play it end to end for timing; then dress the Square and the Lanes to final quality for the vertical slice.
Draft v0.1. Real names and dates (Roanoke 1587–1590, the Dare family, the Flannan Isles keepers, the Mary Celeste) to be checked again before release; the real people who appear are shown with respect, never as monsters. Companion pages: Chapter 1 Blueprint, Game Design, Storyboard.
