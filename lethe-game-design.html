How LETHE Plays

The story is the spine. This document is the muscle: what the player holds, what they fight, what it costs, and the moments they'll talk about afterwards.

Third-person cinematic action-adventure Unreal Engine 5 PS5 · Xbox Series · PC Target rating: Teen / PEGI 16 Part One: 12–15 hours
01

What kind of game this is

Think The Last of Us and God of War (2018) for the closeness of the camera and the weight of the fights, Uncharted for the set pieces, A Plague Tale and Hellblade for the dread, and Control for the strangeness. One continuous camera in Lethe, no cuts during play. Fights are close, heavy and deliberate, never button-mashing.

30%Combat
30%Exploration and puzzles
15%Stealth and escapes
25%Story, choices, the THEN timeline
Tone rule: violence is real but never gory. Creatures of the Grey break apart into ash, ink, plaster dust or fog. In the NOW timeline Nysa never kills a human being: against Sealer agents she hides, escapes, disarms or knocks out. This keeps the game open to a wide audience while the story stays adult.
02

The chapter loop

Every chapter, from Roanoke to Göbekli Tepe, follows the same eight beats, so players learn the rhythm and feel the dread of the last one coming.

  1. Dream

    A short playable dream, sent from the layer below. It shows the place before you see it.

  2. Arrive

    Fall into the new layer. New light, new language, new rules.

  3. The forgotten

    Meet the people who live there. Help them, and they give you memories back.

  4. Eesa's star

    Find the star he carved. Always a puzzle; often a fight to reach it.

  5. THEN

    Play as Eesa in the same place, years earlier. What you do as him changes what Nysa finds.

  6. Set piece

    The chapter's big action moment: a chase, a flood, a siege.

  7. Warden

    The boss that guards the way down.

  8. The price

    Spend one memory you love to open the door to the next layer.

03

Nysa's arsenal

She is a paintings conservator, not a soldier, so every tool she fights with comes from her trade or from the layer she is in. That's what makes her fighting style unlike any other hero's.

Main weapon · melee

The Mahl Stick

A painter's rest stick from her studio: 1.1 m of ash wood with a padded tip. In Lethe she caps it with whatever she finds, and it becomes a fighting staff.

Moves
Light and heavy combos, sweeping strikes, a parry that opens enemies up, a vault over low cover.
Upgrades
A Roman bronze ferrule (Ch. 3), a Hittite iron core (Ch. 5), each changing its weight and speed.

From Scene 1: it leans against her easel.

Power weapon · costs memories

The Brass Chisel

The Sealers' own tool, stolen from Tomas's desk when she escapes the clinic. It cuts through the Grey, but every strong strike is paid for with a memory.

Carve
Breaks armour of plaster and stone in one blow.
Unname
Strips the name off a creature of the Grey, and it falls apart.
Anchor
Carve a name into a place to make it safe: a checkpoint and a refuge.

From Scene 12 (taken) and Scene 16 (first used).

Tool · light

The UV Lantern

Her conservation UV torch. In our world it shows overpaint. In Lethe it shows what has been hidden: erased writing, secret doors and enemies that can't otherwise be seen.

Uses
Reveal hidden enemies, stun creatures of the Grey, read erased inscriptions, solve light puzzles.
Limit
A charge meter that refills only at anchored places.

From Scene 1: the UV check on the painting.

Ability · voice

Calling the Names

Like the Roman general who called his soldiers by name across the river Limia, Nysa can shout a name she has learned.

Uses
Rally the forgotten to fight beside her for a short time, part water, break a boss's hold, wake a frozen person.
Limit
Each name can be called once per area. Learning names means talking to people.

From Scene 16: she calls Eesa's name across the Shoal.

Relic weapons: one per layer

Each layer gives her one weapon of its own time. It is historically accurate, and it can't leave the layer: when she goes down, it stays behind (things belong to their time). Every chapter feels new, and players miss what they lose.

04

The price: memories as power

This is the system no other game has, and the one that makes LETHE more than an action game. Nysa's memories are real items in her journal, collected through the story (the car journey in Scene 3 gives four). Her strongest moves cost them.

  • Spending is permanent. A spent memory is deleted from the journal, and the game changes: spend "Abbu's laugh", and in every later flashback Abbu's laugh is silent.
  • You choose which one. When the chisel needs a memory, the player picks from their own journal. Small ones buy small strikes; precious ones buy the strongest.
  • No grinding. Memories are limited. New ones come from helping the forgotten: give a lost person their name back and they give you a memory in return.
  • One memory per door. Going down to the next layer always costs one memory the game chooses for you, at the end of each chapter.
  • It shapes the ending. How much of Eesa you still remember when you reach him decides what you can say to him, and how Part Three begins.
05

What you fight

The Grey: creatures of forgetting

Common

Hollows

Forgotten people who stopped whispering their own names. Slow, many, and they grab: if one holds you, it drains a memory. Break the grip, or call its name to free it.

Fast

Smudges

Blurs at the corner of your eye. Invisible except under the UV Lantern. They strike from behind and teach players to use their light.

Armoured

Whitewash

Shapes coated in thick white lime plaster, like overpaint on a painting. Ordinary blows bounce off. Crack them with heavy strikes or one chisel Carve.

Hunter

Lethe Hounds

Dogs made of river water. Blind; they hunt by sound. The stealth enemy: walk, don't run, and never splash.

Elite

Erasers

Tall and faceless. Their touch erases things from your screen: your map, your health bar, your journal flicker and vanish until you kill them.

Human

Sealer Wardens

Masked hunters in Lethe with long chisels and chains. They fight like Nysa, and they learn: use the same move too often and they counter it.

In our world: the Lindqvist Institute

Grey-coated orderlies with sedative darts and handheld tone emitters that make Nysa drowsy (the screen blurs and slows). These sections are stealth and escape: the clinic at night (Scene 12), the dig at dusk (Scene 15). Nysa can knock out, never kill.

06

The Wardens: one boss per chapter

Bosses are not real people. Each one is the layer's way of forgetting, given a body. Every fight has a twist that uses the chapter's history.

Ch. 1 · Roanoke · 1590

The Bellringer

A giant in the fog whose head is the colony's bell. Every time it rings, you forget something mid-fight: a button stops working, the map goes blank. Win by carving the colonists' names into the CROATOAN post until the bell can't ring.

Ch. 2 · Baghdad · 1258

The Drowned Scribe

A towering figure made of the books thrown into the Tigris. The arena floods with ink in waves. Save books from the river to make platforms; burn the rest with fire arrows.

Ch. 3 · Rome · AD 211

The Censor

Its arms are chisels, and it erases faces from the walls around you. Where faces vanish, the floor gives way. Restore the faces, the way Nysa cleans a painting, to make places to stand.

Ch. 4 · Alexandria · 48 BC

The Pyre

The library fire, alive. Each phase you choose: fight, or save the scrolls the fire is reaching for. What you save comes back in Part Three.

Ch. 5 · Hattusa · c. 1177 BC

The Great Forgetting

Not one creature but a storm: an army of the erased crashing against the burning walls of the Hittite capital. A siege, fought on the walls, across the city, into the citadel.

Ch. 6 · Amarna · c. 1336 BC

The Hands of Noon

A blinding sun-disc whose rays end in hands, as on Amarna's own carvings. Its light erases shadows, and the only cover is shade. The reunion with Eesa comes straight after.

Recurring rival: Tomas Vey. Nysa never fights him in Part One; he watches, and lets her go. In Part Two the Sealers send their best Warden after her, and in Part Three Tomas finally steps into the arena himself. The Chapter 7 boss, the thing under the First Door, stays secret until Part Three.
07

Playing as Eesa

The THEN timeline is a different way to play, and it grows with him. Each chapter he is older: 14 at Roanoke, the scholar of the locked portrait by the time Nysa finds him.

His style

Stealth, stars and cleverness

He starts as a frightened boy with no weapons. He survives by hiding, climbing and thinking: reading the stars to find paths, building traps, using what each layer gives him. He fights more as he grows, with a staff and a sling, but he never becomes a brawler.

His tool

The Astrolabe

A brass astronomer's instrument he finds in Baghdad's House of Wisdom (Ch. 2). Aim it at the sky of a layer to reveal hidden routes, time doors to the stars, and solve the game's best puzzles.

Cross-time puzzles

What he does, she finds

Where Eesa carves a star, Nysa finds it. If Eesa breaks a bridge, Nysa has to climb. If he hides a key, she can use it. Every chapter has at least one puzzle solved across fifteen years.

The Bleed

When the timelines touch

Rare moments when both timelines play on one split screen, and the two of them almost meet. The first is in Chapter 3. They are the scenes players will clip and share.

09

Growing stronger

Skill tree

Craft

The Mahl Stick: new combos, a stronger parry, counter-attacks. Paid for with materials from each layer.

Skill tree

Memory

The Chisel: cheaper strikes, new carvings, longer-lasting anchors. Paid for with names returned to the forgotten.

Skill tree

Voice

Calling names: more allies, louder calls, new uses. Paid for by learning the names of the people in each layer.

Collectibles all mean something: Eesa's notebook pages, star charts, and history cards that tell the player what really happened in each layer and mark clearly what is fact and what is our story.

10

For every player

  • Three difficulty settings: Story (for players who want the tale; fights are forgiving), Balanced, and Grounded (enemies hit hard and the Lantern drains fast).
  • Puzzle help comes from inside the story: Eesa's notebook gets more specific the longer you're stuck.
  • Accessibility: full control remapping, subtitles with speaker names, and the violet/blue timelines also marked by shape, not only colour.
  • Languages: each layer speaks its own language with subtitles. The game launches in English, Urdu, Turkish, Arabic and the major European languages.

What we build first

A vertical slice: about 20 minutes of the game at final quality, to show players, publishers and funders. It proves both halves of LETHE.

Slice part 1 · story and feeling

The Store Room (Scene 4)

One room in Chandsar at night. Phone torch, light switch, the stars on the ceiling, Ammi at the door. No combat. Proves the mood, the characters and the "things stay" mystery.

Slice part 2 · action

The Bell in the Fog (Chapter 1)

The Roanoke colony: one fight against Hollows and Smudges with the Mahl Stick and Lantern, one chisel strike that costs a real memory, and the chase as the bell begins to ring. Proves the combat and the price.
