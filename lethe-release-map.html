Release Map

Seven layers, seven chapters, three parts. How the story of LETHE is released, what each part gives away, and how we know when to make the next one.

The three rules

Every part earns the next

The search is the engineNysa doesn't find Eesa until the end of Part Two. Until then, players know him through what he left behind.
Answer one question, ask a bigger oneEach chapter solves its mystery and opens a deeper one, the way each Harry Potter book does.
The audience decides the budgetA part only gets bigger once the previous one proves people want it.

The descent

Lethe is layered like a dig

Archaeologists dig down through layers, and the deeper the layer, the older it is. Lethe works the same way. The recently forgotten lie near the surface. The deeper you go, the older the forgotten, and the more you have to forget to get there.

Eesa went all the way down, looking for the Spring in the Dark. Nysa follows him one layer at a time. Each layer is one chapter, one real moment in history, and one of the seven stars he left behind.

Every chapter gives three things: a clue to Eesa, a truth about the Sealers, and a memory Nysa has to spend.

Eesa's seven stars

Eesa left one star in each layer. Together they form the Big Dipper. The last two, Merak and Dubhe, are the real "pointer stars": a line through them points to the North Star. Players find Merak at the end of Part Two and Dubhe in Part Three, and only then does the map point to the Spring. It's one puzzle that runs across the whole saga, and it's real astronomy.

Measuring the audience

How we'll know people want more

Before each release, we decide what success looks like. Afterwards, the numbers tell us whether to go bigger, fix things, or rethink.

Players

How many people download and start Chapter 1. Steam and itch.io show this for free.

Completion

What share of players reach the end. It shows whether the story holds people, which matters more than downloads.

Wishlists

How many add the next chapter or part to their Steam wishlist. The best single sign of future sales.

"Remember them" sign-ups

The final screen asks "Will you remember them?" and offers a sign-up to hear when the story continues. It fits the theme and builds a mailing list.

Reviews

The share of positive Steam reviews, and what players say they loved or disliked.

Choices

Optional, anonymous statistics on story choices ("62% of players spent the memory"). Useful for design and fun to share with fans. No personal data.

Between the parts

Keeping the audience warm

Gaps between parts are where most series lose their players. LETHE fills them with material that deepens the world without spoiling what's next.

  • The "Erased" video series: one episode per layer's real history, then more.
  • Prequel fiction about the Sealers, set centuries before Nysa. It reveals nothing about the main mystery.
  • Small free side stories: for example 30 minutes as Geta in the Court, or one night of Tomas's work.
  • Star-map theories: fans will try to predict where the next star is. Never confirm or deny.
