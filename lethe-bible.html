Franchise bible v0.326 Oct 2026Working titleConfidential draft

LETHE

Everything humanity forgot went somewhere. Some of it was forgotten on purpose.

Some things were forgotten for a reason.

About the name. We first called this Firstlight, but IO Interactive's 007 First Light came out in 2026, and we can't share a name with a Bond game. LETHE is the working title. "Firstlight" stays in the story as the name of the gift that lets someone see across. The first spend is a trademark search on the final name.

01 · The promise

A hidden war, older than history

For twelve thousand years a secret order has buried temples, burned libraries and chiseled names off stone to keep humanity from remembering a world that sits right behind ours. Now satellites, archaeology and digital archives are uncovering the doors faster than the order can close them.

This is the pull every lasting franchise shares: ordinary people discover a hidden truth about the world, and must decide what they're willing to lose to protect the people they love.

History has holes in itEvery mystery points at something real. Players can look it up, and that's how the lore spreads.
Power costs memoryEvery great act burns something you remember. The cost is real, in the story and in the game.
No one is cleanThe order that protects us has burned libraries. The monsters were once people history wronged.
The forgotten are peopleLethe isn't a dungeon. It's a civilization of the erased, with kingdoms, politics and grudges.

Target rating: Teen / PEGI 16. The darkness comes from ideas and dread, the way Stranger Things works, not from gore. That keeps younger players in while giving adults the weight of The Witcher.

02 · The world

Lethe, the country of the forgotten

In Greek myth, the dead drink from the river Lethe and forget their lives before they're reborn. The Greeks only had half of it. Lethe is a country lying alongside ours. Everything humanity forgets drifts there. What was erased on purpose arrives whole, and remembers why.

The four laws of Lethe

  1. What is forgotten goes to Lethe. Lost cities, dead languages, abandoned gods, the unnamed dead.
  2. Power is paid in memory. Every use of Lethe's power burns a memory from whoever uses it. Small acts cost small memories. Great acts cost the ones you love.
  3. What the living name, the Grey cannot take. Naming a place, a person or a thing anchors it. This is why the Sealers chisel names away.
  4. The dead drink and move on. The erased cannot drink. Someone with no name can't forget and can't be reborn. They're stuck, and they've been waiting for centuries.

Regions

The Shoal of NamesThe crossing

Grey shallows where Lethe meets our world. Names float in the water like leaves. The newly forgotten wash up here, confused, still holding car keys and phone chargers.

The Chiseled CourtSeat of the erased

A palace built from defaced statues and scraped-off inscriptions, ruled by monarchs history condemned to vanish. Its politics are the most dangerous thing in Lethe.

Hattusa of the Thousand GodsThe fallen kingdom

The Hittite capital, abandoned in our world around 1180 BC, stands intact here. The Hittites really did call their land "the land of a thousand gods". Most of those gods are still here, starving.

Alexandria BelowThe library that lived

Every scroll ever lost, shelved in a city of lamplight. Its librarians trade in memory and answer to nobody.

The GreyUnclaimed land

A fog of pure forgetting between the regions. Things that wander in lose their names. Things that live in it never had any.

The First DoorBeneath everything

Under Lethe's mirror of Göbekli Tepe lies the first door ever opened, and the first thing ever shut away. Nobody alive has seen it.

Thin Places

Doorways between the worlds open where something was deliberately forgotten: a cellar bricked up after a crime, a village drowned for a reservoir, an excavation pit, a station platform taken off the maps. Thin Places let the story happen anywhere on Earth, including the player's own city.

03 · The secret history

Twelve thousand years of burying the truth

The Sealers are an order older than any religion, hidden inside every empire, church and government. They never rule. They advise, they archive, and when they think it's necessary they make the world forget. Events tagged Real happened. Our story explains why.

c. 9500 BC

The First Door. Hunter-gatherers raise Göbekli Tepe, a temple older than farming, and touch Lethe for the first time.Real

c. 8000 BC

The burial. The temple ends up under earth. In our story, the people who buried it became the first Sealers. Archaeologists still argue about whether the burial was deliberate.Real

c. 1350 BC

The heretic king. Pharaoh Akhenaten abandons Egypt's gods for a single sun-disk, the Aten. In our story, the Aten was a light from Lethe. After his death his name is struck from the king lists.Real

c. 1177 BC

The Collapse. Within decades, nearly every Bronze Age civilization around the eastern Mediterranean falls, and raiders called the "Sea Peoples" appear. In our story, this was the last open war between the worlds, and the "Sea Peoples" were the erased coming home. The Sealers end it with the Great Forgetting.Real

48 BC

Alexandria burns. A fire during Caesar's war in Alexandria is the most famous of several losses that eventually ended the library. In our story, its scholars had found the way across, and the fire was no accident.Real

AD 211

The erased emperor. Geta, co-emperor of Rome, is murdered on his brother Caracalla's orders, and his name and face are cut from monuments across the empire. Rome's damnatio memoriae becomes the Sealers' favorite tool.Real

1590

CROATOAN. The English colony at Roanoke vanishes, leaving one word carved into a post. In our story, it's the one Thin Place the Sealers failed to close in time.Real

1994

Rediscovery. Göbekli Tepe is identified as a prehistoric temple, and excavations begin the next year. The Sealers can't bury it twice in the age of photographs.Real

Now

The doors are opening. LiDAR maps lost cities under jungle. Archives go online. Humanity is remembering faster than the Sealers can make it forget, and something under the First Door has noticed.

Writers' room only · do not publish

What lies beneath the First Door. It is the first god, made when the first humans remembered something together. When people stopped remembering it, it starved. Lethe is its stomach, and the Grey is its breath. It feeds on whatever is forgotten, and if enough people ever remember it again, it becomes real in our world.

That's the central dilemma of the franchise. Justice for the erased means remembering them, but every act of remembering feeds the thing under the door. The Sealers are right, and the Sealers are monsters. Both are true.

04 · Characters

The people at the center

Nysa

Protagonist · 25

A restorer of paintings and frescoes who brings faded colour back to life. Fifteen years ago, when she was ten, her big brother was erased: no birth record, no school photo, no memory of him in anyone but her. She has a photograph with four people in it. Everyone else sees three.

Lethe drains colour, and Nysa is the one thing in it that stays vivid. Her Firstlight glows violet and pink and shimmers in the air like glitter. In a world of grey fog she is the brightest thing on screen, and proud of it.

Wants
To bring Eesa back.
Needs
To accept that some things were lost for a reason.
Flaw
She will burn anything, including her own memories, to get what she wants.
Gift
The Firstlight: a memory the world couldn't take from her. She was raised to believe that remembering the wronged is a duty, and she never stopped.
Look
Tall, fair skin, dark brown eyes, dark brown hair with natural curls at the ends, tinted purple. A lavender scarf, a fitted jacket with a little sparkle on the cuffs, glitter nail polish chipped from work.
Details
A silver ring with a red stone she never takes off. A quiet word under her breath before a hard moment. Her grandmother's voice in her memories. Never explained on screen.

Tomas Vey

The man in the grey coat · Sealer · 64

Soft-spoken, kind to waiters, and responsible for erasing more than a hundred people. Tomas has spent his own memories so often that he keeps a notebook of his children's names. He's the first person to tell Nysa the truth, and the most dangerous person in her life.

Wants
For the doors to stay shut until he dies.
Secret
He erased Eesa.
Archetype
Snape, Jaime Lannister: the villain who might be the hero.

Eesa

The erased big brother · 29

Nysa remembers the big brother who built Lego spaceships, stuck glow-in-the-dark stars on his ceiling and solved every puzzle box he could find. He taught her the names of the planets.

The Eesa she finds in Lethe has spent fifteen years there. Lethe has its own sky, made of stars humanity forgot the names of, and he has charted it. He builds machines from forgotten parts, serves in the Chiseled Court's guard, and isn't sure he wants to be found.

Look
Tall and lean, warm wheatish skin, dark brown eyes, black hair worn a little long. Mismatched armour from erased eras, hand-built gadgets, a grey cloak lined with his own star map. His light is deep blue starlight, against her violet.
Detail
A cheap kids' space watch with a rocket on it, stopped at the minute he was erased. He never takes it off.
Puzzles
He left locks, machines and star-map riddles across Lethe, hoping someone would follow. When players solve them, they're solving his mind.
Secret
He wasn't taken. At fourteen he followed a mystery to a Thin Place, and the Sealers erased him to close it. He has never forgiven the world that let him go.

Akhenaten

The Sun King Below

The pharaoh Egypt tried to erase. In Lethe he leads the Return: an army of the erased who want to cross back and be seen. He's warm, brilliant and sincere, and if he wins, the thing under the door wakes.

Belief
"They took our names to feel safe. Let them learn what we learned in the dark."
Archetype
Magneto, Thanos: the villain half the audience agrees with.

Hatshepsut

The Queen Who Was Chiseled

One of Egypt's most successful pharaohs, whose statues were smashed and whose images were defaced after her death. She doesn't want war. She wants her name back, and she'll make any alliance to get it. She is the political heart of the Chiseled Court.

Role
Akhenaten's rival. Nysa's most useful and least trustworthy ally.

Geta

The boy emperor · 22 forever

Murdered in his mother's arms on his brother's orders, then erased from Rome. He commands the Court's guard, sleeps badly and is kinder than anyone expects. Younger players will love him. He doesn't survive the trilogy.

Ione

Last Librarian of Alexandria Below

She answers any question, for the price of a memory. Neutral in the war, she has outlived three Sealer grandmasters, and she's the only one who knows what the First Door holds.

05 · The game

What players actually do

Genre: a third-person action-adventure with story choices, in the same family as The Witcher 3, Uncharted and God of War, with an online co-op mode added after launch.

Core loop

  • Explore real-world dig sites, then cross into their mirror in Lethe.
  • Uncover fragments of erased history, which unlock powers and story.
  • Spend memory to fight, open doors and change outcomes.
  • Choose sides in the Chiseled Court. Alliances change the ending and who survives.
  • Solve Eesa's puzzles: the locks, machines and star-map riddles he left across Lethe for someone to follow.

Signature mechanic: the chisel

Memory is the only currency. Using a great power erases something in the game itself: a journal entry gets scratched out, a map region turns grey, a companion stops recognizing Nysa. Players feel the cost because the game really does forget. This is the feature people will talk about.

Expeditions (online co-op, after launch)

Groups of 3–5 push into the Grey together. Any place they reach and name is anchored for every player on the server, with their name on it. One player in the group may secretly be working for the Sealers. This keeps the shared-world naming and the betrayal ideas from earlier, now inside a serious world.

06 · Story stages

Game one: LETHE

The franchise is planned as a trilogy, with the full ending written before production starts. Game one runs about 25–30 hours.

Prologue

The Fourth Face

At her mother's house, Nysa switches off the light in the "storage room" and sees faint glow-in-the-dark stars on the ceiling that nobody else can see. Days later she's called to a dig in the hills outside Şanlıurfa, in southern Turkey, to clean the carvings on a sealed door. Under the dust she finds her brother's name. Tomas Vey arrives. The door opens from the other side.

Tutorial · 45 min · becomes the playable demo and the reveal trailer

Act I

The Shoal of Names

Nysa crosses and learns the four laws the hard way. She spends her first memory to survive and meets Ione, who tells her Eesa is alive.

First memory spent · first boss in the Grey

Act II

The Chiseled Court

Palace politics between Akhenaten and Hatshepsut. Nysa finds Eesa, and he doesn't want to come home.

Major alliance choice · Game of Thrones–style intrigue

Act III

1177 BC

A recovered memory becomes a playable flashback. You play an ancient Sealer during the Collapse and carry out the Great Forgetting yourself.

Perspective switch · players commit the crime they've been fighting

Act IV

Alexandria Below

The truth comes out: Eesa wasn't taken, and Tomas erased him. To learn where the First Door is, Nysa must trade her memory of her mother.

The hardest memory choice in the game

Act V

The Bleed

Akhenaten's army crosses into Istanbul. Erased people walk the streets, and only those with Firstlight can see them.

Open-city set piece in the real world

Finale

The First Door

Nysa reaches the door with Eesa, or without him, depending on your choices. Something on the other side says her name. The game ends as the door begins to open.

Multiple endings, all leading into game two

Writers' room only · the trilogy's ending

In game three, the only way to starve the god under the door for good is for someone it knows to be forgotten completely, by everyone, forever. Nysa chooses to be erased. The last shot of the franchise is the photograph from the first scene. Everyone can see Eesa now, and the fourth face in the picture belongs to a woman no one remembers.

08 · Where we push it

How players find it

Our advantage is that the lore is real. Every mystery in LETHE points at something people can look up, and history and mystery videos already pull huge audiences on YouTube and TikTok.

The plan, in order

  1. Before any announcement: the "Erased" series. Sixty-second videos about real people and places history deleted: Hatshepsut, Geta, Roanoke, the Bronze Age Collapse. Each ends on a chiseled name and a date. No game mentioned yet. This builds the audience and the brand.
  2. An alternate-reality game. A website with one old photograph. Each week another face fades. Hidden clues lead to real erased places. The community solves it, and the answer is the trailer.
  3. Announce at a major showcase with the 90-second "Fourth Face" trailer: The Game Awards (December), Summer Game Fest (June) or Gamescom (August). Store pages go live the same minute.
  4. Wishlists are the number that matters. Target 100,000 before the public demo and 250,000+ by launch. Steam wishlists are the clearest early signal for sales, store featuring and publisher interest.
  5. Steam Next Fest demo: the Prologue, 45 minutes, ending as the door opens.
  6. Creators get early access: history and lore channels first, then streamers. The chisel moments are made for reaction clips.
  7. Launch on PC and PS5 together, Xbox the same day if funding allows, Switch 2 later. A free web companion, "The Archive", lets fans explore the real history behind the game.

Launch price recommendation: $39.99. Premium enough to signal quality, and below the $70 games it'll be compared with.

09 · Start small

The game we build first: The Fourth Face

We don't start with the $10M game. We start with a short, excellent story game made by a tiny team, and let it prove LETHE.

Many lasting indie hits began this way: Undertale, Stardew Valley, Papers, Please and Return of the Obra Dinn were each made by one person, and Hollow Knight by three.

What it is

  • A 1–2 hour story game covering the Prologue and Act I: the photograph, the dig, the door, the first crossing, the first memory spent. It ends on a cliffhanger.
  • Stylized art with strong atmosphere. No motion capture and no voice acting at first.
  • The chisel mechanic is the centerpiece. A small game built around one strong idea can travel far.
  • Built in Godot: free, open source, no royalties.

For comparison, a full AAA game today often costs $100M–300M or more. LETHE is planned as a high-quality AA game: smaller scope, PlayStation-level presentation, stylized realism rather than photorealism everywhere.

Platform costs to know

  • Unreal Engine 5: free to develop with, then a 5% royalty on gross revenue above the first $1 million per game.
  • Steam, PlayStation and Xbox stores keep about 30% of each sale (Steam's cut drops on very large sales).
  • After the store cut, refunds and taxes, a $39.99 sale typically leaves the studio roughly $22–25.

How to fund it without losing the IP

  1. Stages 0–1: self-funding, angel investors or a small seed round. A vertical slice turns "an idea" into "a deal".
  2. With the slice in hand: pitch platform holders (Sony, Microsoft and Epic all fund or co-market games), story-focused publishers and game investment funds. Apply for Epic MegaGrants and any national game-development grants where the studio is based.
  3. The rule we don't break: trade revenue share for funding, never the IP. Book, series, film and merchandise rights stay with us. That's what makes the next section possible.

11 · Books, series, film

From game to franchise

Games have become one of the most reliable sources of screen franchises: Arcane, The Last of Us, Fallout and Castlevania all started as games, and The Witcher went from books to games to Netflix. The path that works is prove the audience with the game, then adapt. Animation usually comes before live action because it's cheaper and stays closer to the source.

Year 0–1The world goes live

Bible finished. "Erased" video series. ARG. Free serialized prequel fiction about the Sealers.

Year 1–2Slice and announce

Vertical slice, funding, showcase trailer, wishlists. Graphic novel: 1177 BC.

Year 3–4Game one launches

Prequel novel published. Expeditions co-op added. Animated series pitched to streamers.

Year 4–6The world expands

Game two. Animated series. Tabletop RPG and merchandise: the chisel, the photograph, the Sealer sigil.

Year 6+Live action

Game three closes the trilogy. Live-action series or film, with the ending already written.

Why it can go wide: every format gives something different. The game makes you spend memories. The series makes you watch someone else lose theirs. The books tell the Sealers' twelve-thousand-year history. And because the places are real, fans will visit Göbekli Tepe and Hattusa the way Lord of the Rings fans visit New Zealand.

12 · Next 30 days

What happens first

  1. Ground the lore. Agree on the real history and myths behind every part of the world (see the companion Lore Sources dossier).
  2. Lock the name. Trademark search on LETHE (and two backups) in the video game and entertainment classes in key markets.
  3. Design the characters. Character sheets for Nysa, Eesa and Tomas using AI concept tools, then one freelance artist to finalize them.
  4. Write the Fourth Face script. It becomes the game, the demo, the trailer and the pitch.
  5. Prototype the chisel in Godot. A small playable test of memory spending, to prove it feels as strong as it reads.
  6. Start the "Erased" channel. Ten short history videos to test which stories pull the most viewers.
