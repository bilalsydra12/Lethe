The Descent

Seven layers of the forgotten, each older than the last. This is what each one looks, sounds and feels like, and how Nysa gets down to the next.

Each layer is lit by the light its people hadLanterns, oil lamps, moonlight on marble, fire, a false sun. At the bottom, nothing at all.
The deeper, the older the wordsEnglish, Arabic, Latin, Greek, Hittite, Egyptian. The bottom layer is older than writing, so there are no words there. That's why it was forgotten.
Only two colours come from outsideNysa's violet and Eesa's blue starlight. The deeper they go, the more those two lights matter.

The Surface

"A kitchen table, a photograph with four people in it. Then a hillside outside Şanlıurfa at dusk, dust in the air, and a stone door with every name chiseled away except one."

Light

Warm, ordinary, modern: a kitchen lamp, then the orange dusk over the hills. The last normal light Nysa sees.

Colours

Sound

A fridge humming. Wind over the hills. Brushes on stone. Then silence as the door opens.

Landmark

The T-shaped pillars of the dig, and the glow-in-the-dark stars on the ceiling of the "storage room".

How it plays

Quiet exploration: the house, the dig, small moments. Players get to know Nysa before her world breaks.

The way down

The door opens from the other side. Behind it, grey water: the Shoal of Names, where Lethe begins.

The Vanished

"A palisade in the fog. Doors left open, tables still set. People everywhere, quiet, waiting. Everyone in history who vanished without a trace ends up here."

Light

Hand lanterns and candle-glow inside the fog. You never see more than twenty steps ahead.

Colours

Sound

Creaking timber, dripping fog, a bell somewhere that never stops. People whispering their own names so they won't forget them.

Language

Early modern English, and voices from every century of the vanished.

Landmark

The post carved with CROATOAN.

How it plays

Exploring in fog, with a touch of horror. The first use of the chisel, and the first memory spent.

Eesa's trace · Alkaid

His star carved beside CROATOAN. The wood is still pale. He was here, and not long ago.

The way down

The colony well. When Nysa drops a lantern in, it falls for a very long time.

The River of Ink

"Domes of lapis and gold above a river as black as oil. Pages drift past like leaves. On every bank, scholars copy what they can before the Grey takes it."

Light

Oil lamps in every window, reflected in black water.

Colours

Sound

Water, pages turning, quills scratching, people reading aloud, an oud far away.

Language

Arabic and Persian, with Greek and Sanskrit in the translations.

Landmark

The House of Wisdom, standing on the river that carried its books away.

How it plays

The first big puzzle chapter: brass instruments, star charts and machines. Eesa's mind starts to show.

A brass star machine he built from forgotten parts, still turning. A scholar who taught him says he was looking for "a spring at the bottom of everything."

The way down

Into the river. Nysa dives through the ink, and the lost words of a thousand books rush past her.

Colours

Sound

Latin.

Landmark

Court intrigue and choosing who to trust, stealth through the palace, a chase through the flooded forum.

"Shelves rising out of sight in both directions, every scroll ever lost, all lamplit. In the middle, the great lighthouse of Alexandria hangs upside down, its fire burning downward into the dark."

Light

Thousands of small oil lamps, and the lighthouse fire pointing down.

Colours

Sound

Scrolls unrolling, the sea far off, a librarian's footsteps that never come closer.

Language

Greek, with every language the library ever collected.

Landmark

The upside-down lighthouse, and Ione's reading room.

Trading with Ione: every answer costs a memory, and players choose which ones to give up. Library puzzles.

Eesa's trace · Megrez

The scroll he studied for months: the Sealers' own record. It shows that Tomas erased him, and that he went looking for Lethe himself. Megrez is the faintest of the seven stars and the hardest to find.

The Collapse

"Hattusa, capital of a forgotten empire, standing whole and burning forever. Its thousand gods sit in the empty temples, starving. On the horizon, ships with no flags."

Light

Fire. Everything is lit by a city that never finishes burning.

Colours

Sound

Crackling fire, distant drums, wind through stone tunnels, clay tablets cracking in the heat.

Language

Hittite, written in cuneiform.

Landmark

The Lion Gate, a real gateway at Hattusa flanked by two carved lions.

How it plays

The playable flashback: you are an ancient Sealer during the Collapse, carrying out the Great Forgetting with your own hands. The most intense combat in the game.

Eesa's trace · Phecda

His voice, caught in an echo of memory, talking to someone about "opening the way home." Geta falls here.

The way down

Hattusa's real stone tunnel under its walls, the Yerkapı, leads down and keeps going.

The Sun City

"A city of white sandstone under a golden sun that isn't there. Its rays end in small open hands, the way they do in real Amarna art. Down here, the hands reach out and hold you."

Light

A false sun: bright, golden and shadowless after so many dark layers. Beautiful, and wrong.

Colours

Sound

Heat humming, a single held note, voices singing a hymn to the sun.

Language

Ancient Egyptian, in hieroglyphs scraped off and carved again.

Landmark

The Great Temple of the Aten, open to the sky, and Akhenaten's broken throne.

How it plays

The biggest set piece so far: the erased crossing into Istanbul (the Bleed), and the reunion.

The way down

7c. 9500 BC≈ 11,500 years agoPart Three

The First Door

"Total darkness, and giant stone pillars shaped like people, with carved arms reaching around their sides, as they really are at Göbekli Tepe. The only light is the two of them: her violet and his blue. Somewhere below, water is dripping."

Light

None, except Nysa and Eesa. Then, at the very end, the Spring.

Colours

Sound

Silence. Two people breathing. Water dripping. A heartbeat that isn't theirs.

Language

None. This layer is older than writing. Nothing here has ever had a name, and that is why it was forgotten.

Landmark

The ring of pillars around the First Door, and the Spring in the Dark beneath it.

How it plays

Brother and sister together. Some puzzles need both lights, violet and blue, at once: the two kids, grown up, solving it side by side.

The last star · Dubhe

With Merak, it points the way, as the pointer stars do in the real sky. The map is complete.

The way down

There's no further down. Only the door, the Spring, and the choice.

Why this structure works

Every layer is a world players have never seen in a game, rooted in real history. Each one is darker, older and quieter than the last, so the game itself feels like going down. And through all of it, the only colours that don't belong are a sister's violet and a brother's blue, getting closer one layer at a time.
